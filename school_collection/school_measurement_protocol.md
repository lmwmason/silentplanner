# School background sound measurement protocol

1. Obtain school approval and choose anonymous zone identifiers. Record only integrated sound levels. Keep location codes and any mapping to the school private.
2. Use one sound-level meter and the same settings throughout a comparison. If a meter and compatible calibrator are available, record field checks before and after each session and retain the setting, calibrator reading and any drift in a controlled log. Follow device instructions.
3. Use A-weighted equivalent continuous sound level (LAeq) for 60 seconds per observation. Use the same receiver height (for example, 1.2 m), device orientation and placement during repeats. Record placement, nearby surfaces, windows and doors, and indoor/outdoor status.
4. Repeat at least three times at each position during comparable periods. Record class or break as a broad condition, ventilation, wind, rain and unusual events. Consider a separate set for another period. Use a windscreen outdoors where applicable.
5. For a phone app, use the same phone and app settings and record `app_estimate_dBA`. Its microphone, gain, filtering and calibration can differ from a sound-level meter. Keep these estimates separate.
6. Avoid audio recording. If raw audio is necessary for a separately approved study, obtain appropriate school permission, avoid capturing intelligible speech, restrict access and follow the approved retention schedule. Store no names, faces, voice recordings or exact coordinates in this project.
7. Use the CSV template. Check units and calibration status each time. Record background with no study drone present, flag unusual observations in notes, and validate the file before import.

The public regression estimates digital WAV levels. School records describe local background sound, support grouping by measurement conditions and help choose where a follow-up study would be informative.
